Adjustable Hall & 45° Reed Roof

Roof weight and winter snow bear on 45° reed thatch & rafters. Paired struts resolve ridge load into horizontal tie-beam tension H locked by dry birch wedges, while spruce posts carry gravity safely well below Euler buckling. Between outer posts, horizontal birch logs slide in doweled cleats beneath a moss settling gap. Above clean sand, charred spruce girders on fieldstones keep flex under L/500 so the clay floor never cracks.

02 · CALENDAR

Year-Round Natural Building Process (Material Availability Windows)

Why missing the winter freeze, spring sap run, or +5°C lime window doubles work
[ ± ]
JAN – FEB · DEEP FREEZE

1. Reeds, Thin Birch & Snow Haul

Cut standing dead Phragmites water reeds cleanly over frozen ditches and stack in conical stooks. Thin young birch (Ø10–12 cm) and collect deadwood spruce. Peel birch bark the same day, haul heavy poles across snow on a , carve sliding tenons, and dry birch indoors.

Gate #1: Lowest timber sap + dry dead reeds + near-zero friction hauling on snow.

MARCH · THAW & CHAR

2. Clay Test, Charring & Code Check

Ribbon-test local subsoil clay (≥5 cm ribbon without snapping) or choose the route. Debark spruce frame logs once temps cross +2°C. Deep-char (3–4 mm) every girder, outer sill, lower 40 cm of posts, ridge , and gable horns.

Fire shield: Deep char sterilizes wood sugars and slows soil-splash fungal attack.

APR – MAY · PIERS & FRAME

3. Strip Peat, Fieldstones & Solo Bents

Strip organic topsoil to clean mineral sand (stack sods grass-down). Dig 25 cm drip swales. Level flat fieldstones (±3 mm), set charred girders & loose subfloor poles, solo-raise bents with ropes & forked pike-poles, fix 45° rafters, and slot birch wall logs under the skeleton to season.

Drying shed: Standing frame keeps birch bays dry while shrinking.

LATE MAY – JUN · SAP WINDOW

4. Birch Bark, Spruce Roots & Thatching

Harvest wide white birch bark strips (50 cm) and pull shallow spruce roots (split & soak). Lash hazel battens every 30 cm, lay 30 cm winter reed thatch stitched with spruce roots (zero wire), then saddle the ridge with 3-layer bark + 12 cm living sods + crossed charred .

Gate #2 (2–3 weeks): Birch bark peels cleanly ONLY during peak spring sap flow.

JULY · HOT DRY WIND

5. Woven Reed-Clay Floor (J7)

Secure Ø9 cm conifer subfloor poles with woven hazel rods. Pack 8 cm clay-dipped reed pad plus 3.5 cm lean 1:3 sandy clay-ash wear crust, tamp dense, and burnish leather-hard with a wet cobble. Keep crawlspace wide open underneath.

Cure gate: Hot dry July breeze cures earthen floor from both top and underside.

AUG – SEP · WALL CLOSE (≥+5°C)

6. Exterior Clay-Typha OR Slaked Lime

Confirm birch MC <18% and measure settling drop. Harvest non-flowering Typha leaves/stalks (zero fluff). Dip in thin clay slip OR milk and pack 120–150 mm on the EXTERIOR of the warm birch. Mount 30 mm vented cavity (rodent mesh) + boards.

Lime frost gate: Slaked lime MUST cure at ≥+5°C for 4+ weeks to carbonate into limestone.

OCT – NOV · WINTERIZE

7. Removable Crawlspace Skirt

Stack saved peat sods and spruce boughs around the pier perimeter for winter floor warmth, then pull open every April for dry summer airflow.

Seasonal breathing: Warm closed under-floor in winter; dry ventilated timbers in summer.

03 · SOLO TOOLS

Basic Hand Tools for One Person (No Prices, No Power Required)

Everything needed to fell, peel, split, join, thatch, and raise alone
[ ± ]

I · Layout & Level

  • 5 m tape & mason line (squaring bays & diagonal checks)
  • Spirit level or site-made A-frame level (bedding pier stones ±3 mm)
  • Plumb bob & wooden compass (scribing round logs & vertical posts)

II · Debark & Split

  • Drawknife or bark spud (same-day peeling of ~240 birch poles + wedges)
  • Froe (skeltuvas) (forged or cut from a used vehicle leaf spring; splits cleats & boards)
  • Site-carved hardwood mallet + 4 fire-hardened splitting wedges

III · Timber Joinery

  • Medium felling/limbing axe (~1.2 kg) (bucking & rough tenon cheeks)
  • Light carpenter hatchet (~400 g) (one-handed paring of sliding tenons)
  • Bow saw (green & dry blades) + 20–25 mm mortise chisel + sloyd knife
  • Sharpening stone & bastard file (3 min daily honing halves build hours)

IV · Harvest, Solo Lift & Lime

  • Hand scythe / sickle (~750 winter reed sheaves) + spade
  • Site-carved tools: clay tamper (meška), flat thatching paddle, 45 cm wooden needle, , 2 forked pike-poles
  • Ropes & ratchet straps (solo bent-raising & cinching wedge joints)
  • Rubber gloves & eye goggles (mandatory if mixing caustic slaked lime Ca(OH)2)
04 · FREE BOOKS

Free Open Books & Primary Technical Manuals

Verified public-domain treatises, laboratory handbooks, and open university mechanics texts
[ ± ]

Timber & Joinery

  • USDA Wood Handbook (FPL-GTR-282, Free PDF) · World standard on wood mechanics, elasticity E, tangential shrinkage, and decay durability.
  • George Ellis · Modern Practical Carpentry (1906, Archive.org) · Classic public-domain illustrated treatise on traditional roofs, trusses, floors, and timber joinery.

Nordic Timber, Sod & Lime

  • Riksantikvaren (Norwegian Heritage Guides) · Free public conservation sheets on birch-bark sod roofs (torvtak), timber framing, and air-lime mortar (kalkmørtel).
  • Bygg og Bevar (Nordic Building Craft Portal) · Open technical guides on log walls, traditional joints, and natural finishes.
  • Historic England Technical Guidance Hub · Free conservation advice notes on breathable lime mortars and earth walls.

Cattail (Typha) & Moisture Physics

  • Fraunhofer IBP · Cattail (Typha) Building Material · Primary German building-physics research on Typha thermal conductivity (λ 0.048–0.060), capillary drying, and mold resistance.

Structural Mechanics & Geometry

  • Tomasz Wierzbicki (MIT) · Structural Mechanics (LibreTexts) · Free open textbook covering beam bending, torsion, and elastic stability.
  • MIT OCW 2.080J · Column Buckling Notes (Free PDF) · Complete derivation of Euler buckling modes and boundary conditions.
  • Byrne’s Euclid (N. Rougeux) & Bartosz Ciechanowski · Visual geometry and interactive mechanics classics.
